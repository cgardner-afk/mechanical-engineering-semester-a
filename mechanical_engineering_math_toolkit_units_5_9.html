Read the problem and choose a sign convention

Mechanical engineering problems use both size and direction. Before calculating, draw a simple sketch, mark the direction you will call positive, and label each quantity with its unit. A force of −200 N does not mean a force smaller than zero in size; it means a 200 N force acting opposite the chosen positive direction. Similarly, a negative moment describes a rotational direction, not an impossible result.

Worked method: signed quantities

  1. Choose right as positive.
  2. Write the rightward force as +300 N and the leftward force as −180 N.
  3. Add signed forces: +300 + (−180) = +120 N.
  4. Interpret the result: the net force is 120 N to the right.

Check: The answer's direction agrees with the larger force.

Rearrange equations without guessing

Many engineering equations contain multiplication and division. To rearrange a formula, perform the same operation on both sides until the unknown quantity stands alone. Letters are placeholders for numbers, and units provide a useful check.

Worked method: solve for area in σ = F/A

  1. Start with σ = F/A.
  2. Multiply both sides by A: σA = F.
  3. Divide both sides by σ: A = F/σ.
  4. For F = 12,000 N and σ = 60 N/mm², calculate A = 12,000 ÷ 60 = 200 mm².
  5. Check by substitution: 12,000 N ÷ 200 mm² = 60 N/mm².

Second example: For ΔL = FL/(AE), solve for A. Multiply both sides by AE to obtain AEΔL = FL. Divide by EΔL: A = FL/(EΔL). Put the entire denominator in parentheses when using a calculator.

Area and unit conversions

Area is measured in squared units. Because 1 m = 1,000 mm, one square meter contains 1,000 × 1,000 = 1,000,000 mm². To convert mm² to m², divide by 1,000,000. To convert m² to mm², multiply by 1,000,000. A force divided by an area gives stress.

UnitMeaningUseful equivalence
NNewton, force1 N = 1 kg·m/s²
PaPascal, stress1 Pa = 1 N/m²
MPaMegapascal, stress1 MPa = 1,000,000 Pa = 1 N/mm²
GPaGigapascal, stress or modulus1 GPa = 1,000 MPa
mm²Square millimeter, area1 mm² = 0.000001 m²

JJoule, energy1 J = 1 N·m (energy context)
Worked conversion: 250 mm² = 250 ÷ 1,000,000 = 0.000250 m². A 10,000 N load on 250 mm² produces 10,000 ÷ 250 = 40 N/mm² = 40 MPa. Using meters instead gives 10,000 ÷ 0.000250 = 40,000,000 Pa = 40 MPa.

Although N·m and J have the same base units, a moment and energy describe different physical quantities; use the unit name appropriate to the context.

Toolkit 4 of 8

Scientific notation and calculator entry

Scientific notation writes large or small values as a number times a power of ten. For example, 200,000,000 Pa = 2 × 10⁸ Pa = 200 MPa. A typical calculator may show 2E8, which means 2 × 10⁸. It does not mean 2 multiplied by the letter E.

Worked method: use consistent units

  1. Convert 70 GPa to MPa: 70 × 1,000 = 70,000 MPa.
  2. Express strain as a decimal, not a percentage: 0.2% = 0.002.
  3. Use σ = Eε: 70,000 MPa × 0.002 = 140 MPa.
  4. Check that strain has no unit and stress has the same unit as E.

Right triangles and force components

Geometry can help split an angled force into two perpendicular parts called force components. The horizontal part is Fx, and the vertical part is Fy. When angle θ is measured from the positive horizontal axis, use Fx = F cos θ and Fy = F sin θ. Cosine gives the adjacent-side ratio of a right triangle; sine gives the opposite-side ratio. A calculator in degree mode or a supplied ratio can provide their values.

Worked method: a 100 N force at 30° above horizontal

  1. Identify the angle reference: 30° above the horizontal.
  2. Use the supplied values cos 30° ≈ 0.866 and sin 30° = 0.5.
  3. Horizontal component: 100 × 0.866 = 86.6 N right.
  4. Vertical component: 100 × 0.5 = 50 N up.
  5. Check: both components are smaller than the original 100 N force, and both point in the indicated directions.

If the angle is measured from the vertical instead, the opposite/adjacent relationships change. Draw the triangle rather than memorizing which function always belongs to x or y. For torque, use the force component perpendicular to the lever arm.

Equilibrium: force balance and moment balance

Translational equilibrium means the net force is zero, so the object has no acceleration of its center of mass. Rotational equilibrium means the net moment is zero, so there is no angular acceleration. A stationary beam must satisfy both requirements. One equation cannot replace the other.

ΣFx = 0   |   ΣFy = 0   |   ΣM = 0

Worked method: simply supported 4 m beam with 200 N load at 1 m from left

  1. Draw upward reactions RL and RR and downward 200 N load.
  2. Choose counterclockwise moments as positive and take moments about the left support.
  3. Moment balance: RR(4) − 200(1) = 0, so RR = 50 N.
  4. Vertical force balance: RL + 50 − 200 = 0, so RL = 150 N.
  5. Check: 150 + 50 = 200 N upward, balancing 200 N downward.

Choose a pivot that makes an unknown reaction have zero moment whenever possible. At a truss joint, balance horizontal and vertical forces separately; at a whole beam, moment balance can isolate one support reaction first.

Toolkit 7 of 8

Graph slope, area, and square roots

A graph's slope tells how much the vertical quantity changes for each unit of horizontal change. On a velocity–time graph, slope is acceleration: (change in m/s) ÷ (change in s) = m/s². The signed area under a velocity–time graph gives displacement, with units (m/s) × s = m. These are different calculations and should not be confused.

Worked graph example

  1. A cart's velocity rises uniformly from 2 m/s to 6 m/s over 4 s.
  2. Slope = (6 − 2) ÷ 4 = 1 m/s².
  3. Displacement = rectangle (2 × 4) + triangle (½ × 4 × 4) = 8 + 8 = 16 m.
  4. Check: average velocity is (2 + 6)/2 = 4 m/s; 4 × 4 = 16 m.

A square root reverses squaring: √49 = 7 because 7 × 7 = 49. When calculating speed from kinetic energy, solve v² = 2KE/m, then use v = √(2KE/m). Enter the entire quantity inside parentheses before pressing √. Speed is nonnegative; velocity can be signed.

Toolkit 8 of 8

Check your answer and explain your reasoning

Independent engineering work should end with three checks. First, confirm the units make sense: a force should be in N, a moment in N·m, stress in Pa or MPa, and energy in J. Second, check the direction or sign: does a negative result mean a physically meaningful opposite direction? Third, check the size: does doubling an input have the expected effect?

Useful sentence frame: “I selected Design B because it meets the stated cost and deformation constraints and has a lower calculated average stopping force than the other feasible designs. This conclusion assumes the measured stopping times remain valid for the tested conditions.”

Engineering answers are strongest when they include the calculation, units, assumptions, and a short explanation.

Angled force components

A force at an angle θ measured above the positive horizontal axis has Fx = F cos θ and Fy = F sin θ. Enter degrees, not radians.

Simply supported beam reactions

For a beam with one downward point load, Rright = P × a/L and Rleft = P − Rright. The distance a is measured from the left support.

Stress and engineering unit conversion

Stress σ = F/A. When force is in N and area is in mm², the result is directly in MPa. The equivalent value in Pa is one million times larger.

Kinetic and gravitational energy

Explore KE = ½mv² and GPE = mgh. Compare what happens when you double mass, speed, or height. This assumes g = 9.8 m/s².
