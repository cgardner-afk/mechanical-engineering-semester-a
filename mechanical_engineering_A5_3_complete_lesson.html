SECTION 01

Why balanced moments matter

A drawbridge can remain still even while gravity pulls down on it, and a crane boom can support a suspended load without rotating. These systems are not free from forces. Instead, the forces and turning effects are arranged so that the structure has no tendency to accelerate or rotate. Engineers call this condition equilibrium. In this lesson, you will move beyond a single lever and calculate the combined effects of several forces acting at different positions.

Lesson A5.1 introduced the moment of a force, and A5.2 used opposing moments to balance an ideal lever. Here you will organize several moments with positive and negative signs, find the net moment, and calculate an unknown force or distance needed to restore balance. You will also learn that a zero net moment is only one part of complete static equilibrium: the net force must also be zero.

SECTION 02

Choose a pivot and a sign convention

Every moment calculation begins with a chosen pivot. A force's turning effect depends on where it acts relative to that pivot. To combine moments, assign a consistent sign to each turning direction. Throughout this lesson, counterclockwise is positive (+) and clockwise is negative (−). Engineers sometimes use the opposite convention; either is valid when applied consistently.

For a horizontal beam, an upward force to the right of the pivot tends to rotate counterclockwise, while a downward force to the right tends to rotate clockwise. On the left side, those directions reverse: a downward force to the left tends to rotate counterclockwise, and an upward force to the left tends to rotate clockwise. Do not decide the sign from the arrow alone; always identify the pivot first.

SECTION 03

Calculate the net moment

The net moment is the algebraic sum of all signed moments about the chosen pivot. In a two-dimensional system with perpendicular forces, each moment is the force multiplied by its moment arm, with a plus or minus sign indicating rotation direction. If the sum is positive, the net turning tendency is counterclockwise; if negative, clockwise. If the sum is zero, the clockwise and counterclockwise moments balance.

ΣM = M₁ + M₂ + M₃ + …
Rotational equilibrium: ΣM = 0

Worked example 1 · Three forces around a pivot

A horizontal bar has a pivot at its center. A 30 N downward force acts 0.40 m to the left, a 20 N downward force acts 0.50 m to the right, and a 10 N downward force acts 0.20 m to the right. Find the net moment.

  1. Choose counterclockwise positive.
  2. Left downward force: +(30 × 0.40) = +12 N·m.
  3. First right downward force: −(20 × 0.50) = −10 N·m.
  4. Second right downward force: −(10 × 0.20) = −2 N·m.
  5. Add: ΣM = 12 − 10 − 2 = 0 N·m.

The bar has no net turning tendency about the pivot. Whether it is in complete equilibrium also depends on whether its net horizontal and vertical forces are zero; the pivot can provide an upward reaction to balance the three downward forces.

SECTION 04

Find an unknown balancing force

Engineers often know where a force will act but need to calculate its required magnitude. Set the sum of moments equal to zero, substitute the known moments, and solve for the unknown. A free-body diagram is especially useful because it makes the side of the pivot and the direction of each force clear.

Worked example 2 · Counterweight on a beam

A 240 N load acts 0.50 m to the right of a pivot. A counterweight will act downward 0.80 m to the left. How large must the counterweight force be for rotational equilibrium?

  1. Right-side load moment: −(240 × 0.50) = −120 N·m.
  2. Left-side counterweight moment: +(F × 0.80).
  3. Set the sum to zero: 0.80F − 120 = 0.
  4. Solve: F = 120/0.80 = 150 N.
  5. Check: +120 N·m and −120 N·m cancel.

Worked example 3 · Find the placement distance

A 90 N downward load acts 0.60 m to the right of a pivot. A 135 N counterweight acts downward on the left. At what distance should it be placed?

Set moments equal: 135d = 90 × 0.60 = 54 N·m. Therefore d = 54/135 = 0.40 m left of the pivot.

SECTION 05

Solve a multiple-force equilibrium problem

When several loads act on a structure, calculate each moment separately before combining them. Writing a moment table helps avoid arithmetic and sign errors. A force applied directly through the pivot has a zero moment arm, so it does not contribute to the moment equation about that pivot—even though it can contribute to the net force.

Worked example 4 · Balance two loads with one force

Two downward loads act to the right of a pivot: 100 N at 0.30 m and 80 N at 0.75 m. A downward counterweight will be placed 0.60 m to the left. Find the counterweight force.

100 N right100 × 0.30−30 N·m
80 N right80 × 0.75−60 N·m
F leftF × 0.60+0.60F N·m

Rotational equilibrium requires 0.60F − 30 − 60 = 0, so 0.60F = 90 and F = 150 N. Check that 150 × 0.60 = 90 N·m counterclockwise, which cancels the combined 90 N·m clockwise moment.

For assessment questions with three or more forces, write down every moment and sign before solving. A common mistake is to compare only the largest two forces and overlook a smaller force acting at a long distance.

SECTION 06

Rotational equilibrium versus complete static equilibrium

It is possible for a system to have zero net moment but still accelerate in a straight line. Imagine two equal upward forces acting at equal distances on opposite sides of a pivot. Their moments cancel, but their upward forces add. If nothing else acts, the object accelerates upward. Therefore, zero net moment alone does not guarantee that a structure is stationary.

For a rigid body to remain at rest in a two-dimensional static-equilibrium problem, engineers check three conditions: the sum of horizontal forces is zero, the sum of vertical forces is zero, and the sum of moments about any chosen point is zero. We write these as ΣFx = 0, ΣFy = 0, and ΣM = 0. In many beam problems, support reactions provide the forces needed to satisfy the first two conditions.

ΣFx = 0   ·   ΣFy = 0   ·   ΣM = 0

Worked example 5 · Include the support reaction

A weightless horizontal beam has a pivot at its center. A 50 N downward force acts 0.40 m to the left and another 50 N downward force acts 0.40 m to the right. The moments cancel: +20 − 20 = 0 N·m. The downward forces total 100 N. For complete equilibrium, the pivot must exert an upward 100 N reaction on the beam. Then ΣFy = 100 − 50 − 50 = 0.

SECTION 07

Include angled forces and cable tension

For an angled force, only the component perpendicular to the lever contributes to the moment about the pivot. If a force of magnitude F acts at angle θ to the lever at distance r, its moment magnitude is Fr|sin θ|. Determine its sign from the direction of the perpendicular component and the side of the pivot. This is especially important when cables hold a beam or sign in place.

Worked example 6 · Cable supporting a beam

A horizontal beam extends 2.0 m to the right of a wall hinge. A 300 N downward load acts at its outer end. A cable also attaches at the outer end and pulls upward at 30° above the beam. Ignore the beam's own weight. What cable tension is needed to prevent rotation?

  1. Take moments about the hinge. The hinge forces create no moment about the hinge itself.
  2. The load produces a clockwise moment of −(300 × 2.0) = −600 N·m.
  3. The cable's vertical component is T sin 30° = 0.5T, producing counterclockwise moment +(0.5T × 2.0) = +T N·m.
  4. Set ΣM = 0: T − 600 = 0, so T = 600 N.

The cable tension is larger than the downward load because only half of the tension acts perpendicular to the beam. The hinge also supplies reaction forces to balance the remaining horizontal and vertical components. The moment equation by itself does not determine those reactions.

SECTION 09

Apply equilibrium to engineering decisions

Moments determine whether an object tends to tip, twist, or rotate. Engineers use equilibrium calculations when positioning counterweights on cranes, arranging loads on platforms, choosing cable attachments, and designing hinged mechanisms. The same moment method becomes particularly valuable in Lesson A5.4, where we will calculate how supports share the weight of a beam and its loads.

Suppose a loading platform has a fixed pivot and two loads placed at different distances. The total weight alone does not tell you whether it will rotate. A smaller load farther from the pivot can create a larger moment than a heavier load close to the pivot. Engineers therefore record both the force and the moment arm, evaluate the combined moment, and then check the forces on supports.

Good engineering practice includes a clearly labeled free-body diagram, a stated sign convention, a moment equation with every force included, a numerical answer with N·m units, and a check of whether both rotational and translational equilibrium conditions are satisfied.

SECTION 10

Common errors and reasoning checks

The most frequent error is assigning the same moment sign to every downward force. A downward force on the left of a pivot rotates the beam in the opposite direction from a downward force on the right. Another error is forgetting a force or using a distance measured from the wrong reference point. Distances in moment equations must be measured from the chosen pivot to each force's line of action.

Do not conclude that a structure is in complete equilibrium just because ΣM = 0. It must also satisfy ΣFx = 0 and ΣFy = 0. Finally, if a cable pulls at an angle, use its perpendicular component in the moment equation. After solving, substitute the answer back into the equation to confirm that positive and negative moments sum to zero.

INDEPENDENT PRACTICE

Independent practice with worked solutions

Try each problem before opening the solution. Use counterclockwise as positive and assume a weightless beam unless stated otherwise.

A · Two opposing moments

A 40 N downward force acts 0.60 m left of a pivot. A 30 N downward force acts 0.50 m right of the pivot. Find net moment and direction.

Show worked solution

Left: +40 × 0.60 = +24 N·m. Right: −30 × 0.50 = −15 N·m. ΣM = 24 − 15 = +9 N·m, counterclockwise.

B · Three moments

A beam has a 60 N downward force 0.30 m left of the pivot and downward forces of 20 N at 0.40 m right and 25 N at 0.40 m right. Is it balanced?

Show worked solution

ΣM = +(60 × 0.30) − (20 × 0.40) − (25 × 0.40) = 18 − 8 − 10 = 0 N·m. The beam is rotationally balanced.

C · Unknown counterweight

A 200 N load acts 0.45 m right of a pivot. A downward counterweight acts 0.75 m left. What force balances the moments?

Show worked solution

0.75F − (200 × 0.45) = 0; F = 90/0.75 = 120 N.

D · Find a distance

A 150 N load is 0.60 m right of a pivot. A 100 N downward counterweight will be placed left of the pivot. Find its required distance.

Show worked solution

100d = 150 × 0.60 = 90 N·m, so d = 0.90 m left of pivot.

E · Angled cable

A 400 N downward load acts 1.5 m right of a hinge. A cable pulls upward at 30° to the beam at the same point. Find tension needed to balance moments. Use sin 30° = 0.5.

Show worked solution

T(1.5)(0.5) = 400(1.5). Therefore 0.75T = 600 and T = 800 N.

F · Full equilibrium

Two 75 N downward forces act 0.20 m on opposite sides of a central pivot. What upward pivot reaction is needed for complete static equilibrium?

Show worked solution

The moments cancel: +(75 × 0.20) − (75 × 0.20) = 0. Vertical force balance requires R − 75 − 75 = 0, so R = 150 N upward.

G · A force at the pivot

A 500 N downward force acts directly through a pivot. What moment does it produce about that pivot?

Show worked solution

The moment arm is zero, so M = 500 × 0 = 0 N·m. The force still matters to vertical force balance.

H · Evaluate a design

A 100 N load acts 0.50 m right of a pivot. Two downward counterweights act left: 50 N at 0.40 m and 60 N at 0.50 m. Is the beam rotationally balanced?

Show worked solution

Counterclockwise: 50 × 0.40 + 60 × 0.50 = 20 + 30 = 50 N·m. Clockwise: 100 × 0.50 = 50 N·m. Net moment is 0 N·m, so it is rotationally balanced.

VOCABULARY & GLOSSARY

Vocabulary and key concepts

Rotational equilibrium

A condition in which the algebraic sum of moments about a pivot is zero.

Net moment (ΣM)

The sum of all signed clockwise and counterclockwise turning effects.

Sign convention

A consistent choice of which rotation direction counts as positive or negative.

Static equilibrium

A stationary rigid body has no net force and no net moment: ΣFx = 0, ΣFy = 0, and ΣM = 0 in a planar problem.

Support reaction

A force or moment supplied by a support that helps balance the loads on a structure.

Counterweight

A weight placed to oppose the moment of another load.

Cable tension

A pulling force along a cable; its perpendicular component creates a moment about the chosen pivot.

Lesson summary

Rotational equilibrium occurs when the algebraic sum of all moments about a chosen pivot is zero. To analyze a beam or lever, identify each force and its perpendicular moment arm, choose a sign convention, calculate signed clockwise and counterclockwise moments, and add them to find the net moment. Unknown forces or distances can be determined by setting ΣM = 0 and solving the resulting equation. Forces acting directly through the pivot have zero moment about that point, but still affect force balance. Complete static equilibrium also requires ΣFx = 0 and ΣFy = 0. These principles prepare engineers to calculate support reactions, cable tensions, and the stability of loaded structures.
