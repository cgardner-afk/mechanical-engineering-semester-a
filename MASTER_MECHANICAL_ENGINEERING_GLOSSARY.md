# Mechanical Engineering Semester A — Master Vocabulary Glossary

**Purpose:** Canonical definitions for student-facing lessons, practice, and review. When a term is repeated, retain its core definition exactly; add examples or explanations separately. This glossary is based on the A7.1–A7.4 lesson glossaries and is a living course resource.

## Consistency rules

1. Use the canonical definition in each lesson glossary; expand it with examples in the reading, not by silently changing its meaning.
2. Define unfamiliar terms when first introduced. Reintroduce briefly in later lessons.
3. Preserve units, signs, and assumptions in equations. Differentiate related but nonidentical concepts (distance/displacement, speed/velocity, mass/weight, force/momentum).
4. Before publishing each lesson, compare new glossary entries to this file and add only genuinely new terms.

## Important distinctions

- **Distance versus displacement:** Distance counts the full route and has no direction; displacement describes the signed change between the starting and ending positions.
- **Speed versus velocity:** Speed describes how fast an object moves; velocity also includes its direction. Average speed uses total distance, while average velocity uses displacement.
- **Mass versus weight:** Mass is the amount of matter in an object (kg). Weight is the gravitational force acting on it (N).
- **Force versus momentum:** Force is a push or pull (N); momentum is mass times velocity (kg·m/s).
- **Momentum versus kinetic energy:** For a system with negligible external impulse, total momentum is conserved during collision; kinetic energy of carts that stick together is generally not conserved.
